Overview

From the handout

Pythagorean Identities, as we have seen in a previous section, are derived from the Pythagorean Theorem (a² + b² = c²). For example, consider a point P on the unit circle, with coordinates (x, y). If we draw a right triangle (as shown in the figure below), the Pythagorean Theorem says that x² + y² = 1.

But, remember, that the x-coordinate of the point corresponds to cos θ and the y-coordinate corresponds to sin θ. Thus, we get: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Pythagorean Identities are used in solving many trigonometric problems where one trigonometric ratio is given and we are expected to find the other trigonometric ratios. The next two activities will lead us to find the other two Pythagorean Identities.

Theorem: Pythagorean Identities

From the handout

The three Pythagorean Identities are:

  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
  • 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
  • 1 + cot²θ = csc²θ

Note that each of these identities can be written in different forms, which are all equivalent to one another. For example, sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 can be rewritten as 1 − sin²θ = cos²θ or 1 − cos²θ = sin²θ. You will want to become familiar with all these forms.

Note: trig of a sum is not a sum

From the handout

Notice that in the previous example, we saw that sin(π + π/2) ≠ sin π + sin π/2. This is also true for both cosine and tangent as well; that is, if we want to find the cosine or tangent of the sum of two angles, we cannot assume that it is equal to the sum of the two trigonometric functions of each angle. For example, cos(π + π/2) ≠ cos π + cos π/2 and tan(π + π/2) ≠ tan π + tan π/2. The same is true for finding the difference of two angles.

Theorem: sum and difference formulas

From the handout

The Sum and Difference Formulas in trigonometry are used to find the value of the trigonometric functions at specific angles where it is easier to express the angle as the sum or difference of unique angles such as 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°, and 180°. The six main trigonometric sum and difference formulas are:

  • sin(α + β) = sin α cos β + cos α sin β
  • sin(α − β) = sin α cos β − cos α sin β
  • cos(α + β) = cos α cos β − sin α sin β
  • cos(α − β) = cos α cos β + sin α sin β
  • tan(α + β) = (tan α + tan β)/(1 − tan α tan β)
  • tan(α − β) = (tan α − tan β)/(1 + tan α tan β)

Sign check: cosine formula signs

✎ Extra note: not in your handout

Notice the signs flip on the cosine formulas: a sum inside cos gives a minus in the middle, and a difference gives a plus. Sine keeps the same sign.

Theorem: double angle formulas

From the handout

Double angle formulas are used to express the trigonometric ratios of double angles (2θ) in terms of trigonometric ratios of single angle (θ). The Double Angle Formulas of sine, cosine, and tangent are:

  • sin(2θ) = 2 sin θ cos θ
  • cos(2θ) = cos²θ − sin²θ = 1 − 2 sin²θ = 2 cos²θ − 1
  • tan(2θ) = 2 tan θ/(1 − tan²θ)

Observation: three forms of cos 2θ

From the handout

You may have noticed that cos(2θ) has three different forms. This is because we can use Pythagorean Identities to obtain the other forms. For example, suppose we start with cos(2θ) = cos²θ − sin²θ. We can substitute the first cos²θ with 1 − sin²θ (think about how you can rewrite the first Pythagorean Identity, cos²θ + sin²θ = 1). We will then have 1 − sin²θ − sin²θ, which simplifies to 1 − 2 sin²θ. Thus, cos(2θ) = 1 − 2 sin²θ. Using the same method, you can get 2 cos²θ − 1.

Answer (c)

Theorem: power reduction formulas

From the handout

The trigonometric power reduction formulas allow us to rewrite expressions involving trigonometric terms with trigonometric terms of smaller powers. The Power Reduction Formulas are:

  • sin²θ = (1 − cos 2θ)/2
  • cos²θ = (1 + cos 2θ)/2
  • tan²θ = (1 − cos 2θ)/(1 + cos 2θ)

Note: the other half-angle formulas

Theorem: half angle formulas

From the handout

The Half Angle Formulas are (choose + or − depending on which quadrant θ/2 is in):

  • sin θ/2 = ±√((1 − cos θ)/2)
  • cos θ/2 = ±√((1 + cos θ)/2)
  • tan θ/2 = ±√((1 − cos θ)/(1 + cos θ)) = sin θ/(1 + cos θ) = (1 − cos θ)/(sin θ)

Which sign?: the half angle decides the sign

Practice Example 17 · from your notes

Verify the identity (1 − cos(2θ))/(sin(2θ)) = tan(θ) by rewriting the left side using double-angle identities.

Try it, then check.

Hint for Practice Example 17

Answer

Use cos(2θ) = 1 − 2 sin²θ, so 1 − cos(2θ) = 2 sin²θ. And sin(2θ) = 2 sin θ cos θ.
